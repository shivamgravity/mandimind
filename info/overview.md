Overview

Description
With the release of Gemma 4, a new family of open models is officially in your hands. Leverage local frontier intelligence, native function calling, and multimodal understanding to tackle core developer challenges.

The GenAI for Good Track
Best application addressing high-impact challenges in healthcare, agriculture, or civic engagement using Gemma.

The Autonomous Agent Track
Best use of Gemma's native function calling to build local AI agents that interact with external APIs or tools.

Your Goal
Build an AI application powered by Gemma 4 that solves a real-world problem.

Participants are encouraged to leverage Gemma 4's language understanding, reasoning, multimodal, and agentic capabilities to create innovative solutions across any domain.

Projects should demonstrate meaningful use of Gemma as the primary intelligence behind the solution and deliver a functional prototype by the end of the hackathon.

Applications will be evaluated based on:

Gemma Integration (30%) Innovation & Impact (30%) Functionality (20%) Presentation & Writeup (20%)

Projects that do not make meaningful use of Gemma as a core component of the solution may be disqualified from judging.

While your technical execution is vital, the ability to communicate your vision through a compelling writeup and pitch is what will set the winners apart.

1. Kaggle Writeup
The Proof of Work. This report is your technical verification. It must clearly explain the architecture of your project, how you specifically used Gemma 4, the challenges you overcame in this 1-day sprint, and why your technical choices were the right ones. Its primary purpose is to prove to the judges that your demo is backed by real engineering and that your solution can serve as a strong foundational asset.

The Kaggle Writeup serves as your project report. This should include a title, subtitle, and a detailed analysis of your submission. You must select a Track for your Writeup in order to submit. Your Writeup should not exceed 1,500 words. Submissions over this limit may be subject to penalty.

The below assets must be attached to the Writeup to be eligible:

Public Code Repository
The "Source of Truth". Provide a link to a public repository (e.g., GitHub, Kaggle Notebook). The code must be well-documented and clearly show the implementation of Gemma 4. This is non-negotiable and will be used to validate the authenticity of your project.

Your code repository should be publicly accessible and not require a login or paywall. If you use a private Kaggle Notebook, it will automatically be made public after the deadline.

Provide a link to your public code in the section "Attachments" of your writeup, under "Project Links".

Live Demo
A URL or files for your working demo (this can be a hosted web app, an interactive terminal recording, or a fully functional Kaggle Notebook). This allows judges to experience your project firsthand. It should be publicly accessible and not require a login or paywall.

If you are providing a link or files, you can do so in the section "Attachments" of your writeup.

See the naming guidelines if you are creating a new Gemma model variants.

Evaluation
The judging process is designed to be swift and impactful, reflecting the rapid 1-day sprint format of this event. Our panel of experts will evaluate your submissions based on how effectively you utilize Gemma 4 models to build innovative and practical applications.

Rather than expecting production-ready polish, judges will focus on the core functionality, creativity, and real-world impact of your prototypes. Below is the official scoring rubric that will be used to evaluate each Kaggle Writeup and accompanying demo:

Criteria	Description	Points
Gemma Integration (30%)	Did they effectively utilize Gemma 4? Is the model core to the solution?	0-30
Innovation & Impact (30%)	Does the app solve a meaningful problem? Is the approach creative and relevant?	0-30
Functionality (20%)	Does the prototype actually work? Is the demo convincing?	0-20
Presentation & Writeup (20%)	Is the Kaggle Writeup clear? Does it clearly explain the problem and solution?	0-20
